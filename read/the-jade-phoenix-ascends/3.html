Chapter 3 · The Jade Pavilion

The Jade Phoenix Ascends · by Ling Yue

At the foot of the Cold Ridge stood the Jade Pavilion, a crooked little inn where the road to the heavens and the road to the gutter crossed. Its lanterns burned a watery green, and inside, a blind old woman turned her head the moment Xu Wei crossed the threshold.

"Sit," the woman said. "You carry a fire that the sky would rather were out. That makes you either a fool or a fortune, and I have not decided which."

Xu Wei sat. The ember at her collarbone was warm and quiet now, almost asleep. "I need a path up. To the Nine Heavens. Any path."

The old woman laughed — a dry, papery sound. "There is no path. There are only doors, and each door is guarded by an immortal who expects you to kneel. The first of them is Lord Gu, Master of the Jade Pavilion district, who has not refused a bribe in nine hundred years." She slid a small token across the table. "He will find you interesting. That is either a gift or a leash."

The token was cold jade, and carved into it was a single phoenix — wingless, but rising.

Xu Wei turned it over, and the ember at her collarbone stirred, as if it recognized the shape. Outside, the snow had stopped. Somewhere high above, a bell began to toll, slow and deliberate, calling the Heavens to their evening court. She stood, and the wingless phoenix in her palm seemed, for just a moment, to spread its wings.

And above her, the first star she had ever been told not to look at — flickered.
